# 07 Regions

The geography is persistent and mostly handcrafted. The player builds real spatial knowledge: that cracked wall near the entrance eventually breaks; that sealed freight gate opens from the other side; that dangerous central chamber has a maintenance route around it.

## Every region has five layers

| Layer | What the player does |
|---|---|
| **First visit** | Survive, map the main route, collect basic materials |
| **Familiarity** | Learn resource spots, enemy behaviour, alternate paths |
| **Capability** | Use better tools to reach resources and chambers that were out of reach |
| **Mastery** | Build efficient routes, unlock shortcuts, take on rare encounters |
| **Recontextualization** | A later discovery reveals something new in a place you thought you understood |

Not every wall hides a late-game secret, but enough do that the world is worth remembering.

## Region template

Every region doc should answer: what it was built for, its floors, skill focus, materials, enemies, hazard and the tool that answers it, what gets restored, the boss and what it tests, and what the town gains.

## The Old Works (Floors 1–5)

Abandoned quarries, storage chambers, drainage channels, and maintenance tunnels: the area closest to town. It introduces the whole game in miniature.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Was** | Maintenance and drainage for the upper complex, later quarried by Bellwether's miners |
| **Skill focus** | Mining and Smithing, with Fishing and Cooking introduced at Lantern Pool |
| **Materials** | Stone, copper, iron, first cobalt; common cave fish; basic herbs |
| **Enemies** | Scuttler, Bellcap, Slagback, Rivet Sentry, Lampwarden, Sinkmaw ([05](05-combat.md)) |
| **Hazard** | Water surges in the Pump Station |
| **Restore** | The Pump Station, and with it the first working section of the deep lift network |
| **Boss** | The Sluicekeeper |
| **Town gains** | The deep lift, Rank II, forge tier 2, station freight service |

### Floor 1: the Entry Yard

The main lift landing. Lanterns, old carts, a noticeboard, a worker shelter that serves as the first safe room. This is the boundary between town safety and dungeon risk, and where the license test (the tutorial) happens.

A **cracked wall** a few steps from the landing can't be broken until you have an iron pickaxe and Mining 12. Behind it: a sealed storeroom with the first schematic.

### Floor 2: the Broken Galleries

Basic ore, weak enemies (Scuttlers, Bellcaps), and branching routes. The **first locked freight gate** is here, and it only opens from the other side.

**The Irregular chase** happens here on an early trip (see [05](05-combat.md)). The Hollowhorn's escape smashes open a route into the deeper galleries.

### Floor 3: Lantern Pool

A relatively safe fishing area with plants and a stranded maintenance platform out on the water. It should become an early favourite place: quiet, beautiful, a good place to stop.

- The first **shortcut**: an old ladder shaft runs from the west shore straight up to the Entry Yard, but its bottom rungs are gone. Tie on a rope and it becomes a rope ladder, so the daily commute skips the galleries (D16).
- The stranded platform holds the best fishing spot in the region. Reaching it needs a repaired walkway: six planks, two iron ore, and Smithing 2.
- The old maintenance hut on the north shore is a safe room. Tavi has been living out of it.

### Floor 4: the Lower Cut

Better materials and tougher enemies (Slagbacks, Sinkmaws, Rivet Sentries). Here the player first sees a **blue cobalt seam** and can't mine it yet: future progress, visible before it's reachable.

Side tunnels here are pitch dark, and something hunts in them. A lantern upgrade makes them workable.

### Floor 5: the Pump Station

A large mechanical area hit by **water surges**. A rising hum and a gauge needle warn you about three seconds before a surge floods a channel, pushing you and dealing damage. You learn to time your crossings, and valves change which channels flood.

Repairing the station takes three objectives:

1. **Restore power.** Feed cores into the station's cells. Every core you spend here is one you didn't sell.
2. **Clear the intake.** A Sinkmaw brood is nesting inside the main mechanism.
3. **Find the operating sequence.** Bring a control-room plate to Iona to translate the valve order.

Repairing it changes the region: a flooded path drains, a new resource pocket opens, and a freight platform starts working. It also finds the part Oren needs for the first cobalt pick head.

Engaging the main controls wakes the Sluicekeeper.

### Boss: the Sluicekeeper

An ancient maintenance automaton tangled in mineral growth and roots. It isn't evil. Its damaged instructions identify any movement near the pump as contamination.

**Arena:** the main pump hall. A circular basin with raised walkways, pillars, and drainage lanes.

| Phase | Attacks |
|---|---|
| **1** | **Sweeping jets:** horizontal water beams that sweep across lanes. Jump them or break line of sight behind pillars. **Pressure bursts:** floor telegraph circles that erupt a moment later. |
| **2** | The basin floods. Shallow water slows you, and the walkways become the safe lanes. **Slow rotating projectiles:** rings of orbs turning outward, the Bellcap lesson at a larger scale. |
| **Recovery** | After its big attacks it vents steam and its machinery panels open, exposing glowing weak points. |

**What it tests:** lanes (from Rivet Sentries), surges (from the Pump Station), rings (from Bellcaps), and punishing exposed weak points.

**When it falls:** you shut it down rather than destroying it. Its memory core goes to Iona and becomes the first readable record of the lift network. The station controls come back, and every floor marker in the Old Works lights up at once ([02](02-world-and-story.md)). The guaranteed reward is its pressure valve, which Oren and Iona turn into your first resonance tool.

*Later (after the slice):* an optional questline to repair the Sluicekeeper. Afterwards you can see it quietly maintaining the Pump Station whenever you pass through.

### Chapter end

The Rank II ceremony at the Survey Office. Then the whole town gathers to watch an enormous lift descend into the dark for the first time in generations, and there's a festival that night.

### The five layers in the Old Works

- **First visit:** survive the Entry Yard and the Galleries, map the main route, take home copper and stone.
- **Familiarity:** learn the Bellcap rooms, the safe path past the Slagback den, the best spot at Lantern Pool.
- **Capability:** the iron pick breaks the cracked wall, rope opens the quarry drop, the lantern upgrade opens the Lower Cut side tunnels, cobalt becomes minable.
- **Mastery:** open the freight gate from behind, use the ladder drop, bank through Pump Station freight, hunt down the Irregular.
- **Recontextualization:** the floor markers light up. Iona translates the Entry Yard signage and it turns out to be maintenance directions pointing somewhere nobody has found. Later, after the Rootvault, Lantern Pool's water starts flowing from somewhere new.

## The deeper regions *(outlines)*

| Region | Floors | What it was | Focus | Hazard and answer | Town gains |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **The Rootvault** | 6–12 | An underground farming district overtaken by luminous forest | Botany, living pathways, water management, creatures that interact with vegetation | Spores, answered by the filter mask | Better cultivation and medicine |
| **The Drowned Exchange** | 13–20 | A flooded transport and trading district | Fishing, water levels, broken bridges, valuable cargo, machinery under the surface | Deep water, answered by waders and then diving gear | Better transport and unusual goods |
| **The Ember Foundry** | 21–30 | An industrial region still running on failing instructions | Advanced metals, heat and cooling, mechanical enemies, production systems | Heat, answered by a cooling suit | Manufacturing what the town could only salvage |
| **The Quiet City** | 31–40 | An intact residential district | The central mystery becomes personal | TBD | TBD |

**The Exchange Camp** *(new, after DanMachi's Rivira)*: a delver-run camp on a safe floor in the Drowned Exchange. A second hub deep below town, with a bed, traders, and freight, at steep prices. Tavi probably ends up living there.

**The Quiet City:** no obvious battle damage, no bodies where you'd expect them, and the lights still come on at scheduled times. The people who built this place weren't just workers serving a machine. They lived here, and something convinced all of them to leave, or to go further down.

**The Root Engine** lies below all of it.

## Beyond the authored regions

If there's ever a variable or endless mode, it lives below the Root Engine, after the story, and never replaces the handcrafted regions. The game keeps its identity: a world you know, with an unknown frontier still beyond it.
