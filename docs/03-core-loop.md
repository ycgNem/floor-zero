# 03 Core Loop

## At a glance

```
  TOWN (morning)                      DUNGEON
  pick a purpose ──► prepare ──► lift ──► familiar ground ──► the frontier
        ▲                                                         │
        │                                                  return decision
        │                                                         │
  sleep ◄── upgrade, craft, deliver ◄── bank the haul ◄── TOWN (evening)
```

A typical expedition runs **15–30 minutes**. Most sessions should end with a satisfying accomplishment and a clear reason to come back.

## 1. Choose a purpose

Before leaving town you usually have **one main objective and one optional opportunity**:

- Main: get six cobalt fragments for a new pickaxe. Optional: check whether the white cave fish are active at the lower pool.
- Main: open the shortcut to the Pump Station. Optional: gather herbs for a town request on the way.

The noticeboard, residents, and your own goals suggest objectives. You pin up to three on an **expedition card** in the HUD. You should never be juggling twelve chores.

## 2. Prepare briefly

Preparation should matter without becoming admin. It should take under two minutes. Your loadout is:

- a **weapon** and a **secondary** (shield, flare launcher, and similar)
- a **tool belt**: pickaxe, rod, sickle, plus whatever traversal tools you've earned
- up to **four food slots** on the hotbar
- whatever's left of your **pack**

The decisions are trade-offs, not checklists: safer loadout or faster gathering? More healing or more space? Familiar route or exploratory route?

## 3. Descend through familiar ground

Early on this is dangerous. Later you move through it efficiently because you know the enemy positions, the safe paths, the good resource pockets, the escape routes, and where to stop and fish. Mastery should be felt physically in how you move through the map.

Major transport unlocks should let late-game players start near the work they actually want to do, instead of jogging through trivial enemies.

## 4. Push toward something uncertain

The last part of the trip holds the real risk: a new corridor, a tougher resource node, a miniboss, a broken bridge, a rare encounter, or a room you once had to retreat from.

## 5. The return decision

You have useful loot. Do you head back, check one more chamber, spend food to reach a new shortcut, or abandon a side objective to protect the important haul?

The tension is about judgment, not a constantly draining survival meter. The pack is what makes it bite.

### The pack and the haul

- The pack starts at **24 slots** *(tunable)*. Small things like cores, herbs, and bait stack. Ore and fish mostly don't.
- Pack upgrades come from crafting and, later, hired haulers.
- Everything in your pack is **unbanked** until it reaches town storage, or a restored station with freight service that sends it up for you.
- Unique quest items and discoveries (map fragments, schematics) are always safe.

## 6. Turn the haul into a visible improvement

Back in town you craft the tool, deliver the materials, repair the lift part, cook the fish, upgrade your room, or learn what the recovered object means. Then the game shows you a new possibility.

## Defeat: the rescue

**Change:** the pitch said "lose a portion of unbanked resources." Here that happens in-world.

When you go down, you black out. Mara's lift crew, or a passing party, hauls you back to the nearest restored station or to town. You wake up at the inn.

| You keep | You lose |
|---|---|
| All equipment | About **half of each unbanked stack**, rounded down *(tunable)* |
| Skills and XP | A **rescue fee** in money, scaled by depth |
| Town upgrades | |
| Quest progress, unique items, discoveries | |
| Your map and opened routes | |

No corpse runs. No dropped gear to retrieve. Getting home safely should clearly be better, but failure still leaves some progress, and Mara gets a running joke out of it.

## Getting out

- **Walk out** the way you came. Shortcuts make this much faster over time.
- **Station lifts** carry you to town from any restored station.
- **Freight service** at major stations banks your haul without ending the trip.
- **Safe rooms** heal you and let you plan, but they don't bank anything.

There's deliberately no recall item. Reaching a station is the judgment call.

## Days and replenishment

**Change:** the pitch said renewables return "over meaningful expedition cycles." Here a cycle is a day, and **a day only ends when you sleep.**

- Sleeping in your bed starts a new day. There are no real-time timers, and nothing happens while you're offline.
- Renewable things refresh on day boundaries, each on its own cooldown of 1–3 days: ore seams, fish, plants, ordinary creatures, small salvage.
- Each morning the noticeboard lists the day's local conditions: a richer seam, a migrating creature, an unusual fishing opportunity, a blocked minor route, a visiting merchant. **The map stays knowable. The opportunities keep moving.**
- You can make several trips in one day. Time of day is mood, not pressure: you leave in morning light, and after your first trip down the town shifts to evening, lamps lit, when you come back up. That walk home through warm windows is the reward ritual.

### What persists and what renews

| Permanent | Renewable |
|---|---|
| Opened gates, repaired lifts, finished bridges | Fish, plants, mineral seams |
| Cleared story obstacles | Ordinary creatures |
| Defeated major bosses | Small salvage |
| Lit floor markers | Daily local conditions |

The dungeon's mineral processes explain why seams regrow. Wildlife migrates back into territory you've cleared. Plants regrow where conditions suit them.

## Things we don't do

- Hunger or thirst meters
- Constant equipment repairs or durability grind
- Crops dying while you're away
- Daily login rewards or obligations
- Random destruction of your home
- Twelve-chore lists
- Timing minigames for basic gathering. The interesting decision is where and when to gather, not whether you can click a shrinking circle for the hundredth time.
