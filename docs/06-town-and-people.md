# 06 Town & People

Bellwether should feel like home, not a vending machine.

## Building a town for first person

- **Compact.** About two minutes to walk end to end. No fast travel needed inside town.
- **Landmarks visible from everywhere:** the shaft, the main lift's headframe, the big bridge. You should never feel lost in town.
- **Vertical.** Houses stacked on the rim, stairs, bridges over the shaft, freight platforms on chains.
- **The shaft is the centrepiece.** From the bridges you look straight down into the mist. Restored infrastructure is visible from up here: once the deep lift runs, you can watch its lights sink into the dark.
- **Morning and evening.** You leave in morning light and come back to lamps lit ([03](03-core-loop.md)).
- **One signature sound:** the lift bell, rung every time a lift departs. It's where the town's name comes from.

The Survey Office is Bellwether's version of DanMachi's Guild: it licenses delvers, buys cores, and records deeds.

## Residents

Each key resident is tied to a system, remembers what you've done, and has a personal arc that moves forward with the story and the help you give.

### Mara, the liftkeeper

Practical, dry, and unwilling to lower anyone into a shaft she doesn't trust. She runs the lifts and the rescue crew, tracks restored routes and infrastructure, and gets a running joke out of every time she has to drag you out.

*Arc hook:* Mara hasn't been below the first station in twenty years. Restoring the deep lift means she has to decide whether to ride it.

### Oren, the smith

A skilled craftsperson frustrated by the limits of the materials he has. Your discoveries expand what he can make. He runs the forge and its upgrades.

*Arc hook:* Oren wants to make one thing worthy of the builders. Every new tier you bring him gets him closer.

### Pell, the cook

Buys fish, teaches recipes, and treats every newly discovered creature as a possible ingredient until proven otherwise. Runs the kitchen and meal buffs.

*Arc hook:* Pell is writing the definitive cookbook of the dungeon, one species at a time, and the last recipe is somewhere in the Quiet City.

### Iona, the surveyor

Collects maps and fragments of the old language. She starts out wanting facts, then realizes the facts are troubling. Runs licenses, Feats, the core exchange, translations, and the map table.

*Arc hook:* Iona is the first to suspect what the Root Engine really is, and has to decide whether to tell the town.

### Tavi, another young delver

A recurring peer, not a constant rival. They have their own ambitions, mistakes, and discoveries. Tavi turns up in safe rooms, swaps information, and sometimes asks for help.

*Arc hook:* Tavi pushes too deep, too early, and needs rescuing, a mirror of your own first rescue. Afterwards they become an occasional party member for specific expeditions.

## Resident arcs

No relationship meters and no romance (D12 in [11](11-decisions-and-questions.md)). Residents' arcs move forward through the main story and the requests you take on for them. Helping someone pays off in recipes, schematics, discounts, story scenes, and unique gear.

## The town reacts

- After you restore a lift, people talk about the new route.
- After you discover a crop or a fish, it starts showing up in the market and on Pell's menu.
- New residents arrive as routes open and work picks up.
- Dialogue references your rank and your deepest floor.
- The deepest-floor board at the Survey Office shows your name.
- When the deep lift first descends at the end of chapter one, the whole town turns out to watch, and there's a festival that night.

## Town projects

The **Town Works board** lists community projects. Each needs delivered materials and some money, gets visibly built over the next few days, and adds a service or upgrade:

- Repair the east bridge (shortcut across town)
- Build the greenhouse (unlocks cultivation)
- Expand the kitchen (more recipes, better meal buffs)
- A freight crane at the Lift Plaza (station freight service)
- Upgrade the forge (next smithing tier)

This is the Town track from [04](04-progression.md), and it's always visible in the world, never just a number in a menu.

## Your home

You start in a room above the Counterweight. Eventually you can buy a small house or workshop overlooking the shaft. It's a personal milestone alongside the main campaign, and it answers the question: what am I earning all this money for, besides earning more efficiently?

Your house offers:

- storage that works as your bank
- a garden
- crafting stations
- trophy displays: boss trophies, rare catches, recovered relics
- furniture
- **armour stands and a mirror**, so you can actually see your gear in a first-person game

It should visibly express how you play. The fisher has aquariums and hanging nets. The explorer has maps and recovered objects. The smith has tool racks and a ridiculous private furnace.

Nothing bad ever happens to your home. It's where the pressure stops.
